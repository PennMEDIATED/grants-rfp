Requests for Proposals

Penn MEDIATED Grants Program (AY26-27)

The Penn Center on Media, Technology, and Democracy

The 2026-2027 Penn MEDIATED Grants RFP seeks proposals from the Penn academic community for data-driven and computational research of the information ecosystem and its effects on democracy. Penn MEDIATED intends to invest in projects that deploy research tools for broader public impact, including building durable data pipelines, deploying visualization dashboards, developing analytical tools, and producing routine data-driven analyses. This request for proposals (RFP) invites applications for two distinct categories of grants:

  1. 1.

    Research Grants of $5,000-$10,000; and

  2. 2.

    Deployment Grants of $15,000-25,000.

Scope

All grant proposals should focus on the information ecosystem (e.g., traditional mass media, digital news, podcasts, peer-to-peer conversations, elite political speech, influencers, social media, search engines, or generative AI), and its effects on democracy (such as through political speech, public deliberation, censorship, polarization, civic participation, voting, or elections). For a list of previously funded grants, please refer to the Penn MEDIATED Grants in AY2025-2026.

Evaluation Criteria

Grant proposals must be aligned with the RFP’s scope to be considered. Grant proposals will be evaluated by the quality of the proposed work, including the value of the research. Further, the proposals will be evaluated by their prioritization of public impact, such that the research and deployment activities of the proposal have a direct and explicit relationship to improving the information ecosystem and supporting democracy. We also encourage interdisciplinary collaboration, especially applications involving researchers from several schools affiliated with Penn MEDIATED.

Grant Categories

1. Research Grants, $5,000-$10,000

Research grants are intended to support projects using data and/or computational methods to advance the scientific understanding of the information ecosystem and its interactions with democracy. Application of new and emerging empirical methods, gathering and sharing new data sources, and the development of new open-source software, is encouraged but not required. We further encourage the prioritization of impact, such that the research and other activities of the proposal have a direct and explicit relationship to improving the information ecosystem and supporting democracy.

2. Deployment Grants, $15,000-$25,000.

Deployment grants are intended to support developing and launching public-facing data systems or tools that broadly inform the public, journalists, civil society, policymakers, and industry on the state of, and challenges to, the U.S. information ecosystem, and to expand the reach and impact of empirical research conducted at Penn. This may include developing durable and continuous data pipelines that produce datasets usable by the broader community; continuously updated dashboards and data visualizations that directly inform the broader public; analytical tools that expand the ability of others to learn from key datasets; and routinized data analysis products, indices, or reports that produce repeated snapshots of some aspect of the information ecosystem. Proposed projects and data systems may also support the applicant’s own research, however the majority of the funding should be for developing and deploying these data products.

Proposal Requirements

Proposals for the 2026 MEDIATED Research Grants should be submitted as a PDF document which includes the following:

  • •

    A Proposal Abstract (200-300 words).

  • •

    A Project Description (500-1,000 words), including the research questions; justification of this research; and a description of the research methodology. Deployment grant proposals should provide significant detail about the data collection, technical infrastructure, data analysis, and specific data products resulting from the project.

  • •

    A Public Impact Plan (500 words), which specifies a non-academic audience (e.g. the public, policymakers, journalists, industry) for the research or deployment project and describes why this research or data product is valuable for that audience. The plan should discuss specific deliverables that the project will accomplish in order to engage with these audiences.

  • •

    A Budget detailing projected costs and the total funding requested. The proposal must also mention any other sources of financial support.

  • •

    A Timeline of key project milestones and completion.

Guidelines

Please also consider the following guidelines:

Eligibility

Faculty, postdoctoral students, PhD students, research staff, and affiliated scholars from these six Center-affiliated schools are eligible to apply (please note that non-faculty who apply, such as postdoctoral scholars and PhD students, must identify a faculty collaborator or mentor):

  • The School of Engineering and Applied Science
  • The Penn Carey Law School
  • The Annenberg School for Communication and the Annenberg Public Policy Center
  • The Wharton School
  • The School Arts and Sciences
  • The School of Social Policy and Practice
Grant Length

The typical grant length will be between 9 to 18 months, starting on March 1, 2027, although longer and shorter grants may be considered. Unspent funds after the proposed grant period will be reclaimed by Penn MEDIATED, unless an extension is granted.

Limitations

Funding support will typically not cover researcher salaries, but may support data purchasing, data collection, data annotation, purchasing hardware and/or software, software engineering or deployment expenses, cloud computing, research assistants, behavioral experiments, and impact-oriented activities, such as events and dissemination.

Recognition

Projects that are selected for funding must provide recognition of the “Penn MEDIATED Research Grants” on all publications, conference presentations, and may be asked to promote the research in coordination with the Penn MEDIATED.
